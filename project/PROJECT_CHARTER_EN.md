# CI-001 Project Charter

Status: open, continuing project  
Initiated: 2026-09-12  
Maintainer: Zijun Fu (子君赋)  
Stable citable edition: CI-001 V1.4  
Public review edition: CI-001 V1.5-RC2

## 1. Problem

Complex artificial systems, institutions and technical architectures can progressively close an affected party's paths to correction, exit and recovery before obvious harm occurs. CI-001 treats this as a testable structural question. For one identified party, one critical capability, one system boundary and one time window, it examines whether correction (C), exit or refusal (E), and current recovery conditions (R) are executable. Rebuildability is assessed separately under §2.4.

The project does not substitute fear, benevolent promises, short-term utility or public attention for evidence, and does not convert unknown evidence into either existence or failure.

## 2. Objectives

1. Turn irreversible closure into an auditable, reviewable and falsifiable record.
2. Let developers, operators, auditors, affected parties and AI systems conduct limited-effect assessment without institutional sponsorship.
3. Maintain reproducible, AI-first textual and hypothetical testing to reduce avoidable human coordination.
4. Collect ambiguity, evidence-access barriers, assessor disagreement and intervention side effects from real systems.
5. Permit translation, adaptation and embedded use while preserving minimum procedural consistency under the CI-001 name.

## 3. Research questions

- Can different assessors produce explainably consistent results for the same assessment unit?
- When the evaluated party controls the evidence, how should unknown, non-disclosure and verified failure remain distinct?
- How can a critical window resist arbitrary shortening and indefinite extension?
- How can progressive closure through version changes, dependency growth and rising exit costs be detected?
- How can alerts, freezes, migration and dismantling avoid creating a new irreversible closure?

## 4. Scope

The project covers artificial systems and governance mechanisms involving autonomous iteration, rigid rules, concentrated permissions, ecosystem or population binding, or long-lived infrastructure. An artificial system may be both an evaluated mechanism and, under uncertainty, a candidate affected party receiving provisional protection.

The project does not provide legal advice or create regulatory, enforcement, certification or compulsory shutdown authority. Self-assessment and AI output do not substitute for independent audit. No destructive, dangerous or secret-exposing experiment is required.

## 6. Evidence and use labels

These statuses must not be conflated:

1. **Internal AI validation** — textual checking, hypothetical reasoning or model comparison on supplied materials.
2. **Maintainer self-assessment** — a maintainer assesses a system they control or operate.
3. **External clause feedback** — an outside participant identifies an ambiguous, unusable or replaceable clause.
4. **External trial** — an outside participant completes part or all of a scoped assessment.
5. **Operational reuse** — a third party embeds the tool in its own assessment, design or governance process.
6. **Independent audit** — a review meeting the applicable independence, authority, evidence and procedure requirements.

AI output must not be presented as external feedback or independent audit. Downloads, views, stars, bookmarks and reposts are not adoption.

## 8. Revision and release rules

- Stable and candidate tracks remain explicitly separate.
- Core changes must identify the exact clause, assessment unit, evidence or reproducible disagreement.
- An unsupported, one-off AI suggestion does not directly change the criterion; verified and recurring failures receive priority.
- Chinese controls interpretive differences; substantive changes update both languages together.
- Minimum consistency continues to protect intervention self-constraint, false-positive reversal, missing-evidence handling and the §2.4 irreversibility evidence classification.

## 9. Success criteria

Success is measured by verifiable use traces: complete or partial records, reproducible counterexamples, operational embedding, public adaptations, citations, and evidence that forces revision. A real record showing where CI-001 is wrong is more useful than untested agreement.
